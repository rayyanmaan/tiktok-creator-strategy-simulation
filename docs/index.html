What the bench is actually computing

For each of the 2,000 trials it draws a cohort size from Poisson(100), then samples four independent behaviours per viewer — completion, share, like, skip — at probabilities that depend on quality and on the topic's measured multipliers. The score is the weighted empirical rate:

E = 0.40 × completion  +  0.25 × share  +  0.20 × like  −  0.15 × skip

Completion outranks shares, shares outrank likes, skips subtract. Those weights are a stated modelling choice, not a fitted parameter. The two scale factors inside the like and share probabilities (35.0 and 21.0) were solved for so that an average video — q = 0.5 — scores about 0.32, just below the gate. If average content cleared the bar by default there would be no strategy trade-off left to measure.

Slide quality past roughly 0.55 and you can watch the distribution cross the line. That crossing point is the single fact that anchors every result in the study.

Where the four strategies sit

Each strategy is a fixed quality distribution plus a posting rate and a trend appetite. Over 1,000 simulated years each, they end up statistically tied on follower count — and cleanly separated on everything else.

Survival = the run ended with more followers than it started. The trend chaser goes viral least often and survives most often, because three posts a week buys three independent shots at the gate.
